# HEADHEIGHT: synthetic head-to-foot chronometry v2.0

This is an original, generated sensor benchmark. It contains **no measured human or optical-clock observations** and no personal data. Its gravitational relation uses the weak-field, low-speed proper-time approximation. The motivating real experiment is Bothwell et al., *Nature* 602 (2022), DOI 10.1038/s41586-021-04349-7; that paper's data are not redistributed here. NIST's publication record is https://www.nist.gov/publications/resolving-gravitational-redshift-across-millimetre-scale-atomic-sample .

`manifest.csv` links opaque scene IDs to `signals/<id>.npy`. Every array has shape `(4096,12)`, little-endian float32 and 8 hours of equally spaced samples. Channels 0:6 are three optical-link in-phase/quadrature pairs (A, B, reference). Channels 6:12 are vertical inertial acceleration, posture-rate proxy, drifting barometric height, two quadratures of a 0.37 m wrapped range sensor, and normalized temperature. Zero blocks mark unavailable channels; zeros are ordinary possible readings outside missing blocks. Optical phases include unknown polynomial drift, thermal response, stochastic error and dropouts. A generated integer-`2π` slip before conversion to I/Q is observationally invisible; the held-out phase re-lock shifts described below are non-integer and observable. The simulated optical response is amplified for numerical conditioning; it is not a claim about current instrument sensitivity.

`train_targets.csv` contains 1,200 labeled scenes. Four hundred test scenes have no participant-visible targets. Family IDs are disjoint between train and test and identify independent generated sessions; they are not target labels. The 1,200 training arrays and labels match v1.0 exactly. The 400 test scenes are newly generated from independent session families and have new opaque IDs; they are not masked copies of v1.0 test scenes. No private answer appears in the public archive. The archive is stored without deflation because high-entropy channel noise would give little compression.

**Held-out sensor regime.** Each test scene has two disjoint 512-sample (about one-hour) failures placed using only its observed posture-rate proxy, channel 7. Compute `numpy.convolve(abs(channel_7), ones(129)/129, mode="same")`; eligible centers are sample indices 256 through 3839. Choose the largest eligible value, breaking ties by earliest index, exclude centers within 640 samples inclusive of it, then choose the largest remaining value. At the first selected peak, channels 8–10 (barometer and wrapped range) are zero for the 512-sample window. At the second, channels 0–3 (optical links A and B) are zero for its window. After the second window, each of A and B retains an independent unknown I/Q rotation with angle sampled uniformly from `[-π,π)`, representing phase re-lock ambiguity; the reference optical pair remains available. These are stylized faults, not measured failure statistics. Selection uses no hidden target. The windows are observable as zero blocks; solvers may apply the documented transformation to labeled training scenes for augmentation, with their own simulated re-lock angles. The physical height and proper-time targets are unchanged by the sensor faults.

For each scene, `h_000` through `h_127` are head-clock height above the foot clock, in metres, at equally spaced times from 0 to 28,800 seconds inclusive. The clock lead `delta_fs` is femtoseconds of head proper time minus foot proper time. Let `v_i` be the NumPy `gradient` of the submitted 128 heights with spacing `28800/127` seconds. Then `delta_fs = 10^15 * trapezoid((9.80665*h_i - v_i^2/2)/299792458^2, dx=28800/127)`. This 128-node convention defines the benchmark target and consistency score. The time scale is deliberately long enough to make the effect measurable in femtoseconds.
