# HEADHEIGHT: synthetic head-to-foot chronometry

This is an original, generated sensor benchmark. It contains **no measured human or optical-clock observations** and no personal data. Its gravitational relation uses the weak-field, low-speed proper-time approximation. The motivating real experiment is Bothwell et al., *Nature* 602 (2022), DOI 10.1038/s41586-021-04349-7; that paper's data are not redistributed here. NIST's publication record is https://www.nist.gov/publications/resolving-gravitational-redshift-across-millimetre-scale-atomic-sample .

`manifest.csv` links opaque scene IDs to `signals/<id>.npy`. Every array has shape `(4096,12)`, little-endian float32 and 8 hours of equally spaced samples. Channels 0:6 are three optical-link in-phase/quadrature pairs (A, B, reference). Channels 6:12 are vertical inertial acceleration, posture-rate proxy, drifting barometric height, two quadratures of a 0.37 m wrapped range sensor, and normalized temperature. Zero blocks mark unavailable channels; zeros are ordinary possible readings outside missing blocks. Optical phases include unknown polynomial drift, thermal response, stochastic error, cycle slips and dropouts. The simulated optical response is amplified for numerical conditioning; it is not a claim about current instrument sensitivity.

`train_targets.csv` contains 1,200 labeled scenes. Four hundred test scenes have no participant-visible targets. Family IDs are disjoint between train and test and identify independent generated sessions; they are not target labels. Generation varies posture, motion, drift, dropouts and cycle slips. No private answer appears in the public archive. The archive is stored without deflation because high-entropy channel noise would give little compression.

For each scene, `h_000` through `h_127` are head-clock height above the foot clock, in metres, at equally spaced times from 0 to 28,800 seconds inclusive. The clock lead `delta_fs` is femtoseconds of head proper time minus foot proper time. Let `v_i` be the NumPy `gradient` of the submitted 128 heights with spacing `28800/127` seconds. Then `delta_fs = 10^15 * trapezoid((9.80665*h_i - v_i^2/2)/299792458^2, dx=28800/127)`. This 128-node convention defines the benchmark target and consistency score. The time scale is deliberately long enough to make the effect measurable in femtoseconds.

Creator-owned simulator code and private labels are organizer artifacts. The dataset source is the original generator `build.py`; no external dataset license is asserted. The paper is a scientific motivation, not the source of the rows. Redistribution of this generated corpus is authorized by its creator for this task; no claim of NIST endorsement is made.

The public score uses the cube root of the product of `exp(-height_RMSE/0.16)`, `exp(-clock_error_fs/180)` and `exp(-physical_inconsistency_fs/180)`, averaged over evaluated rows. Physical inconsistency is the absolute difference between the submitted clock lead and the 128-node integral of the submitted height curve. Exact references score 1; malformed submissions score 0.
